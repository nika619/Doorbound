02The problem

You said eight minutes.

Isha's shower. It's what she always says.

It took twelve.

It usually does. People plan from the plan, not from last time.

Only 30% finish on time.

When students predicted when they would finish, about 3 in 10 made it by then.

Buehler, Griffin and Ross, 1994: the planning fallacy.

And it's never one person.

Her overrun lands on everyone's door. Three mornings, one gate.

0/4
03The maths

A step is a range, not a number.

Each person's step is a log-normal distribution: never negative, sometimes long. What they say is where it starts.

Every morning teaches it.

Each real "I'm done" pulls the curve towards what happens. Five mornings in, her shower is planned at twelve, and the spread knows she underestimates.

Add the steps together.

A morning is a sum of steps. One distribution with the same mean and variance stands in for the sum (Fenton–Wilkinson), so the answer has a closed form.

Then ask the door.

The share of the curve before the gate is her chance of being ready. Tested against 100,000 simulated mornings: within 2 points.

P = Φ((ln g − μ) / σ)

04The re-plan

"She's still in the shower."

Eight more minutes, at 7:26. Her chance of making 7:48 falls through the floor.

Gentlest changes first.

Breakfast to go costs 1. Dad packing her bag costs 2. The dishwasher waiting costs 3. It searches cheapest first, and only what the family allowed.

The cheapest plan that works.

An exact search, checked against brute force on 1,000 random mornings. Alexa says what changed; one word undoes it.

The same board, inside Alexa+ on an Echo Show. This one is replaying the demo morning at twenty times speed, worked out in your browser by the same code the server runs.
